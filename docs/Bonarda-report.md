Since Bonarda Works is fictional, there's no external data to pull — so the ICP work here means something specific: figuring out which of Bonarda's three worker types (employees, contractors, freelancers) is under the most strain, and which persona within that group we should design for. Here's how I'd build it out.

## Step 1: Segment the workforce

Bonarda Works isn't one customer — it's three overlapping populations with very different relationships to the company:

Segment	Relationship	Engagement pattern	Leverage/voice
Full-time employees	Ongoing, contracted	Continuous	High — internal, on payroll, invested in culture
Contractors	Medium-term, project-based	Repeat but intermittent	Medium — semi-embedded but outside HR systems
Freelancers	Short-term, task/project-based	Episodic, often cross-border	Low — easiest to underserve, easiest to lose


The employees already have HR infrastructure built around them. Contractors and freelancers are the segments most likely to fall through the cracks — and they're also the segment Bonarda most needs as it scales delivery across Africa and Europe. That's where the real problem lives.

## Step 2: Build the ICP

**Primary persona: "Kofi," a repeat freelance delivery consultant**

1. Engaged by Bonarda for 3–5 short projects a year, usually 4–8 weeks each, often remote across a different country than the project lead
2. Not on payroll — every engagement starts from a blank slate administratively
3. Has done good work for Bonarda before, but that history isn't visible to the next project lead who staffs him
4. Paid on invoice, with payment timing that varies project to project and PM to PM
5. Wants to build a longer relationship with Bonarda (more/better work over time) but has no visibility into whether that's happening

## What Kofi actually needs:

1. To not re-prove or re-submit the same information every time he's engaged
2. To know, quickly, what's expected of him and what tools/access he'll have — without waiting on a PM's bandwidth
3. Visibility into payment status instead of chasing invoices over email/WhatsApp
4. Some signal that his track record with Bonarda counts for something — that he's building a relationship, not just doing gigs

**What Bonarda needs from solving this:**

1. Faster time-to-productivity when re-engaging known freelancers (which is cheaper than sourcing new ones)
2. Lower PM overhead — onboarding a repeat freelancer shouldn't take as much PM time as onboarding a stranger
3. Retention of good freelancers, who currently have no reason to prioritize Bonarda over other clients
4. A more equitable experience that doesn't leave non-employees permanently feeling like outsiders

## Step 3: Selected problem

**Onboarding — specifically, the "cold-start" problem for repeat contractors and freelancers.**

Right now, onboarding at Bonarda is almost certainly employee-shaped: heavy, one-time, done once and never revisited. But contractors and freelancers get onboarded repeatedly — every new project resets to zero, even when Bonarda already knows the person. That's:

1. Wasted PM time re-explaining/re-collecting the same things
2. A worse experience for the freelancer than for an employee (directly hitting the "equitable" part of the challenge)
3. A missed opportunity to compound the relationship — problem #10 (re-engagement) is really the mirror image of this same gap

It's narrow enough to build in a hackathon, it clearly names an affected persona, and it connects onboarding, equity, and retention — three of the ten lifecycle areas — through one fix, without trying to boil the ocean.

**Proposed direction for the build:** a lightweight "freelancer passport" — a persistent profile that carries verified skills, past project history, and standing access/preferences forward across engagements, so a PM re-engaging a known freelancer can activate them in minutes instead of days, and the freelancer sees their own history and standing with Bonarda accumulate over time.

Two connected views, in one page:

**PM console** — a staffing roster where Kofi shows up as "Known" with his full history: verified skills, past engagement timeline with client quotes, and a 3-step reactivation flow (confirm scope → auto-fill contract & access → send for e-signature) against a stranger's 9-day onboarding path. Click "Reactivate Kofi for this project."

**Freelancer passport** — Kofi's own view, styled as a travel document: photo page with tier/standing, and a spread of engagement "stamps" you can click to expand. Reactivate him from the PM console, then switch tabs — a new stamp appears live with a "Just added" glow, showing the same action instantly updating his record.

It demonstrates the core value prop directly: what's a form for the PM is a stamp for the freelancer, and the two never fall out of sync.
